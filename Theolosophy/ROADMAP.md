### **On the Nature of Ignorance (Avidya)**

**1. The Locus of Ignorance (Ashraya Anupapatti)** _"Maharaj, we establish that Brahman is Self-Luminous (Svayam-Prakasha) and Pure Knowledge. If this is so, where does Avidya (Ignorance) reside? It cannot reside in Brahman, for Light cannot hold Darkness. It cannot reside in the Jiva (Individual), for the Jiva is the _product_ of Avidya. This implies a logical circularity (Anyonya-Ashraya). Does this not suggest that Avidya is merely a heuristic tool with no ontological status, and if so, who exactly is 'ignorant' right now?"_

**2. The End of Ignorance (Avidya Nivritti)** _"We speak of 'destroying' ignorance through Knowledge. However, is this destruction (Nivritti) real or unreal? If the destruction is real, we have introduced a second reality alongside Brahman (duality). If the destruction is unreal/illusory, then ignorance was never actually destroyed, and Samsara continues. How do we define the ontological status of 'Moksha' without violating non-duality?"_

### **On the Mechanism of Realization**

**3. The Final Thought (Akhandakara Vritti)** _"We say that the mind takes the form of the Self in the final 'Akhandakara Vritti' to destroy ignorance. But the mind and its thoughts are inert (Jada). How can an inert mental wave illuminate the Self-Luminous Subject? Does the thought reveal Brahman, or does it merely remove the obstruction (Avarana) allowing Brahman to shine forth, much like the wind pushing away clouds?"_

**4. The Paradox of Sadhana (Causality)** _"Sadhana (spiritual practice) exists within the realm of Time and Causality (Maya). Moksha is defined as the Timeless and Uncaused. How can a finite cause (my meditation/inquiry) produce an Infinite result? Are we creating Moksha, or are we merely removing the confusion that suggests it was ever lost? If the latter, is Sadhana not also an illusion?"_

### **On the State of the Jnani (The Enlightened One)**

**5. The Problem of 'Prarabdha' (Momentum)** _"We say the Jnani sees the world as a mirage (Mithya), yet his body continues to act due to Prarabdha Karma. If the Jnani has truly realized that he is the Screen and not the Character, who is experiencing the Prarabdha? If the Ego is dead, where does the 'suffering' or 'enjoyment' of that Karma land? Can a burnt rope really bind anything?"_

**6. The Speaker of 'I'** _"When a Jnani speaks and uses the word 'I'—'I am hungry,' 'I am teaching'—to whom does this word refer? It cannot refer to the Absolute Brahman, which has no hunger. It cannot refer to the Ego, which has been dissolved. Is there a 'Shadow Ego' (Dagdha-Pata-Nyaya) that remains functional for transaction, and if so, is this shadow conscious or inert?"_

### **On Cosmology and Ishwara**

**7. The Role of Ishwara (God)** _"In the state of highest Non-Duality (Nirguna Brahman), does Ishwara (the Creator God) merge into Brahman, or is Ishwara recognized as the highest projection of Maya? If I become Brahman, what is my relationship to Ishwara? Do I become the Ruler of the Universe, or do I transcend the concept of ruling entirely?"_

**8. Reflection vs. Prototype (Bimba-Pratibimba Vada)** _"Am I the Reflection of Consciousness (Chidabhasa) in the mind, or am I the Original Consciousness (Bimba)? If I am the Original, I was never bound. If I am the Reflection, I am an illusion and cannot be liberated. Who, strictly speaking, gains Moksha? Is it the reflection realizing it is the Original?"_

### **On Deep Sleep and Bliss**

**9. The Witness of Nothingness** _"In Sushupti (Deep Sleep), we say there is an 'absence of specific knowledge.' We infer the Sakshi (Witness) was there because we wake up saying 'I slept.' But is the Sakshi 'knowing' the ignorance in deep sleep? If Sakshi is a 'knower' of the void, does it not retain a subtle form of doership/agency? How is the Witness different from a subtle intellect?"_

**10. The Nature of Ananda (Bliss)** _"Is the Ananda (Bliss) of the Self an 'experience' of joy, or is it simply the 'absence of suffering'? If it is an experience, it requires a subject and object, which violates non-duality. If it is just the absence of pain, how is it different from the inert existence of a stone? What is the 'positive' nature of Brahman's bliss?"_
